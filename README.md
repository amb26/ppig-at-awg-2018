# ppig-at-awg-2018
Archive of works presented by Guild Brothers for the visit of PPIG to the Art Workers' Guild in 2018.

In 2018, PPIG, the [Psychology of Programming Interest Group](http://ppig.org/), held their annual workshop at the Art Workers’ Guild building in Queen Square, London.

[PPIG](https://www.ppig.org/about/) was founded in 1987 in order to bring together people from diverse communities to explore common interests in the psychological aspects of programming and in the computational aspects of psychology.

The [Art Workers’ Guild](https://www.artworkersguild.org/about-us/) was founded in 1884 by prominent figures of the Arts and Crafts Movement. Its principle of ‘learning by doing’ has spread through art education and has had a worldwide influence.

We believe, following Marshall McLuhan, that such an encounter between craftspeople and technologists could be hugely fertile for our cultures:

> “The serious artist is the only person able to encounter technology with impunity, just because he is an expert aware of the changes in sense perception.[…] The artist picks up the message of cultural and technological challenge decades before its transforming impact occurs. He, then, builds models or Noah’s arks for facing the change that is at hand.”

Guildsmen were invited to respond to the presence of technologists in their midst with postcards or writings, through the [call for postcards](2020/11/30/call-for-postcards/). This site collects their [responses](postcards), as well as [responses from PPIG attendees](responses) to the workshop, in a form which now invites further reflection and response via comments.

The final [PPIG 2018 programme](https://www.ppig.org/workshops/2018-annual-workshop/programme) can be seen at the [PPIG workshop site](https://www.ppig.org/workshops/).
